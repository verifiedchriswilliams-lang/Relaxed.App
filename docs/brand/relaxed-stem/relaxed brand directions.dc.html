Three directions for relaxed
Restraint is the premium signal in all three, so each one is a single idea and nothing else. All lowercase throughout. App Store name is relaxed; the wordmark on the web is relaxed.app — the dot-app is a detachable suffix carrying the accent, so removing it never breaks the logo. Each direction has its own typeface, its own palette and its own mark. None uses a filled circle (Headspace) or a script on soft blue (Calm), and none contains a figure, lotus or mandala.

wide, quiet geometric sans. positive tracking on lowercase is what makes it read expensive rather than app-default.
The mark is the lowercase r cut down to a stem and a shoulder — one continuous stroke, 13 units thick, no terminal flourish. It is the only one of the three that is literally your name, which means it earns recognition fastest and never needs the wordmark beside it. Black tile, bone stroke: the most restrained thing on any home screen, and unmistakably not a wellness app.
Trade-off: a single letter is a crowded space — it needs the black tile and the exact stroke weight to stay ownable.

mono for the mark and all data; figtree carries body copy. the monospace is the credibility signal — this is a system that composes something for you, not a library you browse.
A ring interrupted by a horizontal gap — the held pause between an inhale and an exhale, drawn as absence rather than as a shape. Pure geometry, no letter, no figure. This is the investor-facing direction: monospace and cold slate read as engineered and measured, which is the honest story of a product that generates a session rather than serving a recording.
Trade-off: the coolest and least comforting of the three. The gap must widen at small sizes or it closes up — spec'd above.

humanist grotesk with a visible hand — the single-storey a and open apertures give it warmth without softness. the only direction with any personality in the letterforms.
One arc, 222° of a circle, round caps, open at the bottom centre and symmetrical about the vertical — a breath that hasn't closed. It is the evolution of the concentric rings: same gesture, one stroke instead of four, which is what restraint does to that idea. Sand tile with a near-black stroke is the warmest of the three and the furthest from both competitors — nobody in this category owns bone and rust.
Trade-off: an open arc is the least distinctive shape of the three; the palette does much of the identifying work.
